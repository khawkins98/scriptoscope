INSPIRATION / APPLE NEW MEDIA FORUM, 1995

A whole new way to look at multimedia.

The print ad behind our landing page, and the archive that kept it online.

The ad.

In 1995 Apple took a travelling conference called the New Media Forum to four cities: Cannes (May 3–4), Los Angeles (June 5–6), New York City (June 14–15), and Tokyo (July 6–7). The ad pitches it to “experienced multimedia developer[s], creative professional[s] thinking about getting started, [and] business executive[s] exploring the benefits of this new technology.” Wired appears as a partner, alongside sponsors from the desktop-media trade of the time: Adobe, Macromedia, Radius, DV, Keyboard, InterActivity, Red Herring, Advertising Age, and Creativity.

Apple’s own website announced the Los Angeles stop on May 11, 1995, describing the forum as a showcase of “the technology, the techniques, and the hardware for doing audio, video, CD-ROMs, and multimedia products,” for “musicians, directors, game developers, and consumers.”

We haven’t found the photographer, designer, or agency, or which magazines ran the ad. If you know, please tell us.

What we borrowed.

The landing page takes its layout from this ad: a face cut into overlapping, separately coloured panels on a black field, a large serif headline built on “There’s a whole new way to look at…”, an italic invitation underneath, and a centred, print-style rhythm. In our version each panel is a live window drawn by a different Kaleidoscope scheme, so you can drag it apart and put it back together.

The portrait is not the one in the ad. It is an original AI-generated image, and its provenance and generation prompt are recorded in the repository.

Thanks for keeping it.

The oldest copy of this ad we can find online is in The Mothership’s Apple Advertising and Brochure Gallery, a long-running fan archive of Apple print ads and brochures. Their 800 × 976 scan has been on the site since at least 2006, and it’s the one shown here. Thank you for collecting and keeping these.

The same scan was shared again in 2012 on the French blog We Com’in, in a decade-by-decade round-up of Apple advertising. That was how we found it.

The advertisement remains the work of Apple Computer and its original creators. We show it here for credit and historical context only.
