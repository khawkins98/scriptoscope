The original idea.

Greg Landweber and Arlo Rose created Kaleidoscope, the classic Mac OS theming software that made this world of schemes possible. Scriptoscope reads the community’s original scheme files and renders them on the web.

Thanks to Mac Themes Garden, the Kaleidoscope Scheme Archive, and Macintosh Garden for preserving the software, schemes, and their history.

Art & inspiration.

The landing page’s overlapping face panels, dark background, serif headline, and invitation take their art direction from Apple’s New Media Forum: World Tour ’95 advertisement (1995). The original ad was supplied as a design reference; its photographer and designer are not identified in our source material.

The portrait used on this site is an original AI-generated image, created with OpenAI’s image-generation tool on October 18, 2026. It does not depict an identified real person or reproduce the person in the original ad. The generation prompt and asset provenance are recorded in the repository.

An experiment in directing the work.

I built Scriptoscope with a great deal of help from AI agents, using an assortment of Claude, Codex, Antigravity, and Gemini tools. Frankly, I didn’t have the time to do all the procedural diagnostics, reverse engineering, and testing of permutations myself. I probably wouldn’t have had the time to get all my technical skills up to the level that work needed, either.

This is one of the first projects where I’ve done very little of the actual coding myself. Instead, I’ve spent my time directing the development: deciding what it should do, trying the results, and asking for changes. It has been an interesting way to see how much I can direct software development instead of doing all the coding myself—and a pretty satisfactory experience.

That assistance sits alongside the work credited above: the original software, schemes, archives, fonts, and tools that gave this project something to build on.
